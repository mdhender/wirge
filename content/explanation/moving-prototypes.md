A [prototype]({{< relref "../reference/technological-advancement.md#research" >}})
(`PRTO-N`) is the unit that carries a technological level from one place to
another. It is researched at a colony, then either applied with an
[upgrade order]({{< relref "../reference/technological-advancement.md#upgrading" >}})
or moved to another entity. How freely it moves depends on who is holding it,
and that difference is deliberate.

## Why a ship's reach is limited

A colony may hold a prototype of any level. A ship may not: it accepts a
*transfer* of a `PRTO-N` only up to one level above its own `TL`. A `TL-4` ship
can take delivery of a `PRTO-5`, but not a `PRTO-6`.

The limit models the idea that a crew can only handle technology a short step
beyond what it already operates. A prototype far above a ship's level is not
something its people can receive, stow, and keep working — it would be a crate of
machinery they cannot service. A colony, with its full industrial base, has no
such ceiling.

This is why the rules distinguish two verbs that sound interchangeable. To
**accept** a transfer is to take voluntary delivery, and that is what the
one-level rule bounds. To **carry** is merely to hold something already aboard,
and a ship may carry a `PRTO-N` of *any* level — but only one it took by force.

## Capture sidesteps the ceiling

A raid captures a `PRTO-N` regardless of its level. A `TL-4` ship that
raids a colony's storage can come away carrying a `PRTO-10` even though it could
never have accepted that prototype as a transfer. Capture is the one path that
puts a far-higher prototype into a ship's hold.

The exact handling of a captured prototype whose level outruns the ship — whether
it can later be used, passed on, or only stored — belongs with the raid order,
which is not yet written. The point here is the asymmetry: force reaches where a
transfer cannot.

## A logistical trap

The two rules together produce a consequence worth planning around. Suppose a
transport carrying a `PRTO-10` breaks down beside a friendly `TL-6` ship. The
prototype cannot simply be handed across: a `TL-6` ship accepts a transfer only
up to a `PRTO-7`, so a `PRTO-10` is ten levels of machinery it cannot receive.
The stranded prototype is, by the transfer rules, stuck.

The only way to move it to that neighbor is for the neighbor to **raid** it —
the same mechanic an enemy would use. A player who has pushed a high-level
prototype far out on a thin-`TL` ship has, in effect, made it recoverable only
by capture — and capture is not a sure thing, since a raid can destroy the very
prototype it was meant to seize. The lesson is to carry prototypes on ships whose `TL` keeps them
within transfer range of the help you expect to need, or to accept that
recovering a stranded high-level prototype means raiding your own ship.
