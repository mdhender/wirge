Epimethean Challenge rewards players who think like statesmen and economists, not
just generals. Diplomacy and economics — far more than military force — govern
most of what a player does over the course of a game. Understanding why this is
so helps explain how the game is meant to be played.

## Why economics comes first

At the start of the game, every player inherits the same set of pressures:
overpopulation, diminishing resources, heavy competition from neighboring
nations, and the unsolved problem of interstellar travel. None of these are
problems that military strength can answer. A nation that cannot feed its
population, sustain its industry, or fund the research needed to leave its home
planet will fail regardless of how many forces it can field.

This is deliberate. The early game is an economic contest fought within a
crowded home world, where a player's neighbors are competing for the same finite
resources. Decisions about production, population, and research compound over
many turns, so the advantages — and mistakes — made early tend to define a
player's position long afterward.

## Why diplomacy matters

Because the home planet holds as many as 25 nations, no player acts in
isolation. The nations on a planet rule themselves individually yet together
form a single "race," and their fortunes are entangled whether or not they
cooperate. Negotiation, alliance, and the management of rivalries are therefore
core mechanics rather than optional flavor. A player who can secure resources,
trade, or security through diplomacy avoids spending scarce production on
conflict.

## How the focus shifts

As the game develops, a player's challenges move off planet and increasingly
involve other races spread across the star cluster's 100 solar systems. Military
considerations grow more prominent in this later phase — but they are built on
the economic and diplomatic foundation laid earlier. Force is most effective in
the hands of a player who has already won the contests of production and
alliance; it is rarely a substitute for them.
